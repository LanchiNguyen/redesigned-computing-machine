Matrix — production-ready UI at startup speed

Matrix connects fans with creators and digital brands through rewards and gamified engagement. Working directly with the CEO, I turned product specs and a given style guide into high-fidelity, dev-ready screens — in a week.

OVERVIEW

Unlike my zero-to-one projects, Matrix was an exercise in disciplined execution: someone else's product spec, someone else's style guide, a real dev team waiting. The job was to move fast without letting the craft slip — lo-fi to team review to pixel-final, twice over, in seven days.

FEATURE 01
EVENTS & VOTING

The CEO wanted fans to browse live events and vote for their favorite creators. From the product specs I drew low-fidelity mockups, refined them with the team, then produced the high-fidelity screens for handoff:

  • Events — explore live and upcoming events and see which creators are involved
  • Voting — pick a champion per squad, watch live results move, and jump into the stream between creators
  • Small print that matters — votes lock at event end, the navbar swaps Market for Tournament while an event runs, and every screen carries a language toggle
FEATURE 02
TOKENS & NFTS

Matrix also lets fans buy and sell creator tokens and collectibles. Same rhythm — lo-fi with the team, then high-fidelity:

  • Market — track prices and trade tokens and collectibles from creators and brands
  • Orders — filled and open orders in one list, with one-tap cancel for open ones
  • Detail pages — per-creator token and NFT views with price history, attributes, related news, and buy/sell flows
  • Checkout — pay by card or crypto, designed around whichever payment processor the team lands on
PROCESS

Both features ran the same loop: read the spec, sketch low-fidelity wireframes, pressure-test them with the CEO and team, then build the final screens inside the brand's existing style guide — dark surfaces, neon accents, and all. Production-ready meant real states, consistent components, and nothing the developers would need to guess about.

WHAT I TOOK AWAY

Constraints are a feature: a fixed spec and a fixed style guide turn design speed into a craft problem, not a chaos problem.
