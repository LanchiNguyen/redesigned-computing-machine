Mug — rewards for bringing your own cup

A mobile app that incentivizes people and small cafés to cut single-use cup waste — no branded bottle to buy, no special hardware. Just bring whatever mug you already own.

OVERVIEW

Four of us spent a semester building an app for a cause we cared about: sustainability and local business. Unlike other reusable-cup apps, Mug doesn't make you buy a specific cup — any mug counts. You get rewarded, the café gets a customer, the landfill gets nothing.

PROBLEM

People are uninformed and unmotivated about single-use cups, which pile into real climate and material waste. Meanwhile local cafés lack leverage against the beverage giants whose loyalty apps keep customers locked in. Both problems share an answer: make the sustainable choice the rewarding one.

RESEARCH

Using Qualtrics, we surveyed 60 college students who don't use reusable cups. The barriers weren't ideological — they were practical: forgetting, indifference, and hurry. So we reframed the work with How–Might–We questions: how might we encourage coffee drinkers to bring a cup, better advertise local shops, and foster sustainable habits beyond the counter?

“How might we make being sustainable the convenient choice, not the extra chore?” THE HMW THAT GUIDED THE DESIGN
PERSONA

From the research I developed our guiding persona: a student who grabs coffee most mornings, usually in a hurry, familiar with apps like Starbucks' — motivated by saving time and money first, sustainability a close second. Convenience had to win every trade-off.

JOURNEY

A user journey map surfaced where the app could actually touch a morning: the night-before reminder, the walk to class, the queue, the register moment.

IDEATION

A competitive analysis across coffee, rewards, and reusability apps showed the gap clearly: everyone rewarded buying; nobody rewarded bringing. From there we mapped user flows and wireframes around the features that mattered:

  • Accounts that track every cup saved, over time and per café
  • A social feed so friends can see (and nudge) each other's savings
  • Saved payment for faster ordering and instant discounts
  • Custom avatars — a personal mug, made yours
  • A barcode front and center, because the register moment is the whole game
  • Recommended local cafés to spread the love beyond the chains
FEEDBACK

Testing an interactive wireframe, even people with less-sustainable habits were willing to try Mug for the rewards — the incentive framing worked. Two changes came straight out of those sessions:

"Sendable links with first-time promos would get more people to download it."
USER TESTING · ADDED REFERRAL LINKS
Card reloads felt like a chore, so we cut them for direct payment.
REVISED · ONE LESS STEP AT THE REGISTER
BRAND

I created and finalized the brand identity. Greens carry the sustainability story, yellow brings the happiness, red says look here for the moments that matter. Recoleta Bold headlines for its friendly, retro warmth; Montserrat Alternates for soft, readable body text — a palette tuned to the calm of a good café.
