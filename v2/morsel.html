Morsel — eat with your eyes

Designing trust and safety into photo-first food discovery: a self-directed concept exploring how visual discovery can remain immediate while making dietary constraints, evidence quality, and failure states explicit. The idea in one line: safety context follows the dish — from discovery through ordering.

ROLEProduct design, interaction design, prototyping
SCOPE0→1 mobile product concept
STATUSFunctional interactive prototype · not shipped
TEAM · YEARIndependent · 2026
BUILDHTML/CSS/JavaScript prototype · AI‑assisted with Claude Design and Fable
The betPeople decide what to eat with their eyes — and they will trust a photo-first app more, not less, if it is blunt about what it knows: what's in a dish, how good the evidence is, and where its coverage ends. Stated as a hypothesis, not a research finding.
The principlesLead with appetite — the photo is the interface; metadata earns its way in. Preferences rank; allergies protect — taste re-ranks, lifestyle filters, allergies exclude and flag; the tiers never blur. Never imply more certainty than the evidence supports.

APPETITE IS EMOTIONAL.
CHOOSING SAFELY IS NOT.

Food discovery apps optimize for the scroll: beautiful photos, fast decisions. But the moment a diner has a real constraint — a nut allergy, a vegetarian kitchen, a browser tab in a city the app doesn't cover — most photo feeds either go quiet or quietly lie. The feed was the easy part. The system around it was the work.

DECISION 1 ·
PREFERENCE VS
PROTECTION

v1 had one flat “dietary needs” list: Vegetarian sat next to Nut allergy as equivalent chips — a category error; one is a preference to satisfy, the other a hazard to manage. v3.1 splits the model into behaviorally different tiers: lifestyle choices filter the feed and loosen in one tap; allergies are a hard rule with different mechanics everywhere the dish travels. No reset or “clear filters” path ever touches allergy settings.

How allergy context follows a dish. Conflicting dishes leave feed and search; anything already saved is retained and flagged rather than silently deleted; a dish opened directly (from a save or shared link) carries a warning explaining why it's still visible; and the order sheet repeats the warning and locks every delivery and pickup option until the diner explicitly acknowledges the conflict — the checkbox is never pre-checked.

DECISION 2 ·
EVIDENCE WITHOUT
MANUFACTURED CERTAINTY

Star ratings compress bias, self-selection, and revenge reviews into one unfalsifiable number. Morsel proposes a different signal: “X% would order again,” counted from repeat orders through the app's own handoffs — sample size always visible, method one tap away. When a dish is too new for honest math, the design abstains: “New on Morsel — no score yet.”

DECISION 3 ·
APPETITE VS
INFORMATION SCENT

A photo-only feed maximizes appetite but hides price, distance, and name behind a tap. Labeled cards, hybrid density, and a split model were all explored; the selected direction keeps the grazing feed photo-only and gives search results full labels — searchers are comparing while browsers are grazing. That tradeoff is explicitly unresolved until usability testing; it is the first task in the plan.

RESILIENCE —
THE UNGLAMOROUS
PATHS

The prototype implements honest states for the paths most concepts skip. Location off relabels the feed to “For you,” discloses that distances are from a demo neighborhood, and disables “Nearest” sort. No-coverage never shows one city's dishes under another city's name. Zero-result recovery names each active limit — and clearing filters never touches allergies.

ACCESSIBILITY —
AS IMPLEMENTED

Implemented behavior, scoped to what the prototype actually does: semantic controls with aria-pressed/aria-selected/role="alert" where state matters; no nested interactive elements; a keyboard-only pass executed on the critical order path (saved tile → detail → save toggle → order → acknowledgement → provider) with the order sheet as a named modal dialog — focus trapped while open, Escape to close, focus restored to the trigger; a live region for save announcements; ≥44px touch targets; and a 140% text-scale mode that reflows without loss. Not claimed: WCAG conformance or an assistive-technology audit — screen-reader evaluation with real users is still owed.

WHAT REMAINS

PROPOSED There is no Results section because there are no results: no usability sessions have been run. What exists is a testing plan with two rounds — a five-session discovery round, then a dedicated safety round with participants who manage real food allergies plus a domain reviewer — where any misreading of the allergy promise is a fix-first finding.

  • Does photo-only browsing survive real decision pressure — or do users bounce to details just to check price?
  • Does the trust metric read as evidence — or as decoration?
  • Does the acknowledgement gate read as care — or as friction?
REFLECTION

The sophistication isn't in the feed — it's in deciding when the interface stays quiet, when it explains itself, and when it interrupts momentum. Appetite gets silence; evidence gets a visible method; a flagged allergen gets a full stop.
